# Abstract

This document is the standalone v15 revision and experiment plan for the Nature Communications FMI-CNS manuscript. It preserves the v14 scientific center while correcting the architecture of the cognitive-string proposal and strengthening the paper's formal and experimental claims.

The preserved center is that conceptual functional state spaces are typed node-process systems; first-order projections can collapse states that require different target verdicts; local coherence can stabilize an inadequate equivalence class but cannot reconstruct a distinction absent from the registered state; unbounded growth at fixed cognitive order can outgrow certified integration capacity; reflective representation exposes the residual but is not itself an order lift; and another functionally non-equivalent composition of the external functions that spans conceptual space changes cognitive order and can reverse the current fragmentation regime. Recurrent failure to realize, govern, distribute, and regeneratively propagate required order lifts remains a conditionally admissible post-intelligence Great Filter mechanism, not an unconditional identification of the Great Filter.

v15 adds the following load-bearing developments.

First, semantics is made endogenous to the functional state space. The semantic identity of a node is constituted by the metric of its FSS, the typed interactions connecting it to other nodes, its relative position in the global configuration, and the relevant boundary, admissibility, fitness, and continuation conditions. A concept is not assigned an external prose meaning after its mathematical representation has been built. Its meaning is represented by the isomorphism class of its pointed typed metric-interaction structure under admissible transformations. The same structural principle applies in conceptual space and in the P-space generalized by the Functional Model of Adaptation, without asserting object or substrate identity across spaces.

Second, cognitive string theory (CST) is no longer specified as a replacement theorem prover or a complete bespoke verdict engine. CST supplies the semantic mathematical object: a typed metric-interaction representation of reasoning paths, revision families, boundary conditions, transport, and invariant-preserving deformations. Lean supplies proof checking. The primary formal pipeline is therefore:

$$
\text{Article and Supplement}
\longrightarrow
\text{CST/FSS mathematical specification}
\longrightarrow
\text{Lean definitions and theorem statements}
\longrightarrow
\text{kernel-checked conditional coherence}.
$$

Third, the constitutive metric is separated from an optional scalar action. A metric, typed interaction structure, and admissible transport may be semantically load-bearing. A particular Polyakov-style action or weighted defect sum is not automatically load-bearing. It is retained only as an optional search, comparison, or compression device and must satisfy explicit invariance, non-arbitrariness, and utility tests.

Fourth, the paper's theorem package is decomposed into definitional lemmas, substantive mathematical theorems, finite decision theorems, and empirical mappings. The cardinality increment of cognitive order is labeled as a definitional increment lemma. Separate theorems must establish representation invariance, finite span certification, queue-like fragmentation instability, emergence of locally coherent regions under locality assumptions, reversal under added service capacity, and recurrence under renewed growth. This prevents a true-by-definition increment from carrying the scientific burden of the CNS claim.

Fifth, v15 adds a formal bridge to the author's published dynamical CNS. The published contraction-closure-invariance fixed-point regime and the current order-lift formulation are treated as complementary objects. The bridge is conditional: an order lift can enable a globally contracting, compositionally closed, invariance-preserving update operator on an expanded domain; conversely, a newly realized dynamical CNS operator can witness an order lift when its implementation requires a non-equivalent spanning composition unavailable at the prior order. Neither direction is asserted without its premises.

Sixth, the propagation experiment becomes transfer-first. Tests that merely detect invariants built directly into the formalism are diagnostic controls, not primary evidence. The primary endpoints are independent reconstruction of the theorem dependency chain, correct distinction between proof and external premise, application to hidden claim families, recovery after controlled mutation, and regenerative retransmission to a second receiver without author repair.

Seventh, the v15 completion gates are partitioned. The core formal-computational paper is not blocked by an unvalidated scalar cognitive action. Separate gates govern: (i) the core FMI-CNS theorem package; (ii) the endogenous semantic geometry; (iii) Lean proof closure; (iv) string-specific comparative utility; (v) receiver propagation; and (vi) physical and Great Filter mappings.

The v15 objective is not another architectural expansion. It is execution of one narrow phase transition: converting the manuscript's globally distributed conceptual dependency structure into a self-contained, semantically faithful, Lean-checkable mathematical object, then testing whether that object improves independent propagation.

# 1. Executive recommendation

## 1.1 Primary revision objective

The next version should be organized around one executable claim:

> The FMI-CNS-Great Filter relationship can be represented as a typed metric-interaction mathematical object whose semantic identities are endogenous to the represented FSS; Lean can then verify whether the formal conclusions follow from the registered definitions, constitutive assumptions, and explicit external premises.

The revision must not build a second proof assistant. It must create the mathematical specification, theorem statements, proof terms, dependency report, mutation suite, and receiver protocol required to use an existing proof assistant reliably.

The Article should retain one continuous formal mechanism:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{typed conceptual growth at fixed cognitive order}\\
&\rightarrow \text{projection-induced conflation and false semantic identity}\\
&\rightarrow \text{growth of target-relevant integration obligations}\\
&\rightarrow \text{local competence with globally unresolved burden}\\

$$
\boxed{

$$

## 1.3 Core architectural separation

v15 must preserve the following separation:

- **FMA/FMI** defines the functional-state-space ontology and cognitive operations.
- **CST** represents the metric-interaction semantics and admissible reasoning histories mathematically.
- **Lean** checks propositions and proofs about the represented object.
- **The registry** serializes identities, definitions, dependencies, warrants, and manuscript locations.
- **Experiments** test finite realizations, formalization fidelity, receiver propagation, and external mappings.
- **External evidence** determines whether physical, artificial, historical, or civilizational systems instantiate the formal premises.

No one layer may silently perform the function of another.

## 1.4 Positive claim ordering

Every manuscript section should use the following order:

1. State the positive structural claim.
2. Define the mathematical object that realizes it.
3. State the preservation or falsification condition.
4. State the current warrant.
5. Identify unresolved external tests once, without reorganizing the section around negative disclaimers.

This prevents scope qualifications from leaving the opposite of the intended claim as the effective message.

## 1.5 Default submission positioning

The default title should remain centered on the formal mechanism:

*First-order conflation and recurrent fragmentation generate a cognitive near-singularity in reflective conceptual systems*

The Article should foreground the strengthened formal mechanism and Lean-checked dependency package. The string-specific action and physical-force mapping should enter the central Article only if their own gates close. Otherwise, they remain in the Supplement as formally specified hypotheses whose failure does not defeat the core theorem package.

# 5. Foundational FSS ontology and endogenous semantics

## 5.1 Functional state space

A typed metric functional state space is:

$$
\mathcal X=(V,E,\tau_V,\tau_E,g,\pi,\beta,\mathcal A),
$$

where:

- $V$ is the set of represented functional states;
- $E$ is the set of typed interaction or transition instances;
- $\tau_V$ assigns node types;
- $\tau_E$ assigns interaction/process types;
- $g$ is the metric or metric family relevant to the FSS;
- $\pi$ is an optional coordinate or embedding representation whose invariant content is relative position under $g$;
- $\beta$ records boundaries, certificates, provenance, successor obligations, residuals, and continuation conditions;
- $\mathcal A$ is the family of admissible transformations and trajectories.

The metric may be primitive, induced by interaction structure, or jointly defined with it. The plan must state which is used in each formal result.

## 5.2 Nodes and processes

A represented concept or functional state is a node $v\in V$. A reasoning or physical interaction is a typed edge, hyperedge, path, or process subgraph. Definitions, claims, functions, proofs, experiments, and manuscript passages are roles carried by nodes and processes, not additional primitive ontological kinds.

A function has:

- an intensional node containing identity, domain, codomain, and contract;
- an extensional family of process instances that execute that contract.

## 5.3 Endogenous semantic profile

For a node $v\in V$, define its complete semantic profile as the pointed structure:

$$
\operatorname{Prof}_{\mathcal X}(v)
=
(\mathcal X,v).
$$

Its semantic identity is the admissible-isomorphism class:

$$
\operatorname{Sem}_{\mathcal X}(v)
=
[(\mathcal X,v)]_{\cong_{\mathcal A}}.
$$

An admissible isomorphism $F:(\mathcal X,v)\to(\mathcal Y,w)$ must preserve the target-relevant components declared for the assessment:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&F(v)=w,\\
&\tau_V(u)=\tau'_V(Fu),\\
&\tau_E(e)=\tau'_E(Fe),\\
&g(u_1,u_2)=g'(Fu_1,Fu_2),\\
&F\text{ preserves incidence, orientation, boundaries, certificates, and continuation obligations},\\
&F\text{ intertwines the relevant transition and fitness relations}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This definition makes meaning mathematical and relational. A prose label is an expression carrier, not the semantic identity itself.

## 5.4 Target-relative finite semantic signature

A complete pointed FSS may be too large for finite assessment. For target family $T$ and resource budget $B$, define the minimum target-complete support:

$$
K_T(v;\mathcal X,B)
=
\min_{K\subseteq\mathcal X}
\left\{
K:
T\text{ is decidable for }v\text{ from }K\text{ within }B
\right\}.
$$

The finite semantic signature is:

$$
\operatorname{Sem}_{T,B}(v)
=
[K_T(v;\mathcal X,B),v]_{\cong_{T,\mathcal A}}.
$$

This is an assessment projection of the semantic object, not a new definition of meaning. It must return undetermined when no target-complete finite kernel is certified.

## 5.5 Semantic equivalence theorem target

Construct and prove:

> **T-SEM-EQUIV.** If $F:(\mathcal X,v)\to(\mathcal Y,w)$ is an admissible target-preserving pointed isomorphism, then every invariant target proposition expressible in the registered language has the same truth value at $v$ and $w$.

This theorem supplies the foundation for encoding-stable Lean verdicts.

## 5.6 Semantic non-equivalence witness

A semantic difference is certified by a finite target witness when there exists an invariant proposition $\Phi$ such that:

$$
\Phi(\mathcal X,v)\neq\Phi(\mathcal Y,w).
$$

A shortest or minimum-cost witness is useful for receiver reconstruction but is not required for logical non-equivalence.

## 5.7 Conceptual space and P-space

The FMA relation should be stated positively:

> In any FSS, functional identity is constituted by metric position, typed interactions, and admissible relational configuration. Conceptual space and P-space instantiate this structural principle on different operands and substrates.

The cross-space claim is an invariant-level correspondence. It does not identify a concept node with a material node as the same object. It asserts that the same class of mathematical structure can constitute functional identity in both spaces.

## 5.8 Topology induced by interactions

For each operation $o$, define the admissible-transition relation:

$$
R_o\subseteq V\times V.
$$

The family $\{R_o\}$ induces or constrains topology through reachability, connectedness, support overlap, commutation, and boundary preservation. The topology is therefore not merely assigned after the interactions are specified. It is partly generated by which transitions are possible, which commute, and which require global support.

## 5.9 Fitness and continuation semantics

Where participant or system fitness is relevant, augment the semantic profile with:

$$
\mathcal F=(F,\preceq,\operatorname{Viable},\operatorname{Continue}),
$$

and require admissible semantic maps to preserve the declared fitness-order and continuation structure. This prevents identical graph geometry from being treated as semantically equivalent when the participant-indexed consequences differ.

# 6. Reflective FMI subspace and signal boundaries

## 6.1 Reflective subspace

Let $\mathcal C$ be a conceptual FSS. The reflective FMI subspace is the typed substructure:

$$
R_{\mathrm{FMI}}\subseteq\mathcal C
$$

containing representations of:

- concepts and their definitions;
- reasoning-process identities and contracts;
- function identities and execution instances;
- compositions and span claims;
- targets and fitness comparisons;
- proofs and countermodels;
- experiments and measurement protocols;
- warrants and defeat conditions;
- governance and amendment rules;
- signal-boundary residuals;
- unresolved external premises.

## 6.2 Reflective-subspace theorem

> **T-RSUB.** Every registered object required to state, prove, test, amend, or transmit the FMI-CNS-Great Filter relationship can be represented as a concept node, a process edge/subgraph, or both. No additional primitive represented-object class is required.

Proof construction:

1. Map every noun-like registered role to a concept node carrying identity and contract.
2. Map every transformation, inference, execution, comparison, registration, transmission, or amendment to a typed process.
3. Represent multi-input operations by hyperedges or contract-preserving process subgraphs.
4. Represent proofs as premise-to-conclusion subgraphs with proof certificates.
5. Represent experiments as protocol, execution, measurement, and interpretation subgraphs.
6. Represent governance as typed selection and amendment processes over registered objects.

A defeating example must identify an indispensable represented object that cannot be represented in these forms without losing its contract.

## 6.3 Representational strata versus cognitive order

A cognition may reify a lower-stratum process as a higher-stratum concept. Repeated reification creates reflective strata. These strata improve observability and typing but do not count as cognitive order unless they enable another functionally non-equivalent composition of the external functions to span conceptual space.

## 6.4 Signal carriers

Signals belong to boundary spaces $\Sigma_i$, not automatically to the internal conceptual FSS $X_i$. For participant $i$:

$$
\operatorname{Register}_i:\Sigma_i\rightharpoonup X_i,
\qquad
\operatorname{Express}_i:X_i\rightharpoonup\Sigma_i.
$$

A signal becomes a concept only after typed registration. An internal concept becomes externally available only after expression.

## 6.5 Four transmission residuals

For a composition executed by participant $i$ and reconstructed by participant $j$:

$$
R_{i\to j}
=R_{\mathrm{reg},i}
\oplus R_{\mathrm{expr},i}
\oplus R_{\mathrm{recv},j}
\oplus R_{\mathrm{recon},j}.
$$

The direct sum preserves failure source and type:

- $R_{\mathrm{reg},i}$: source structure unavailable after initial registration;
- $R_{\mathrm{expr},i}$: internal structure omitted or altered by expression;
- $R_{\mathrm{recv},j}$: expressed structure not registered by the receiver;
- $R_{\mathrm{recon},j}$: registered structure not reconstructed into an executable contract.

High exposure can coexist with fatal reconstruction residual.

## 6.6 Registration closure

A formalization is registration-closed for target family $T$ when every target-relevant semantic invariant in the manuscript has a canonical mathematical representation and every manuscript use maps to that representation without unresolved ambiguity.

Registration closure is separate from proof closure. Lean can verify a proof about the wrong registered object. The formalization-fidelity audit addresses this boundary.

# 7. First-order projection, conflation, and global disambiguation

## 7.1 First-order and reflective projections

Let $W$ be the latent functional state and define:

$$
q_{\mathrm{FO}}:W\to O_{\mathrm{FO}},
\qquad
q_R:W\to O_R,
\qquad
q_{\mathrm{FO}}=h\circ q_R,
$$

where $h$ removes reflective distinctions.

The first-order projection may preserve domain concepts and ordinary relations while collapsing:

- process identity;
- function contract;
- proof warrant;
- provenance;
- composition identity;
- successor obligations;
- participant-indexed consequences;
- unresolved residuals.

## 7.2 Projection-induced equivalence

Define:

$$
x\sim_{\mathrm{FO}}y
\iff
q_{\mathrm{FO}}(x)=q_{\mathrm{FO}}(y).
$$

A target $\Phi:W\to Y$ factors through $q_{\mathrm{FO}}$ only if it is constant on each equivalence class.

## 7.3 First-order conflation theorem

> **T-CONFLATE.** If $q_{\mathrm{FO}}(x)=q_{\mathrm{FO}}(y)$ and $\Phi(x)\neq\Phi(y)$, no evaluator restricted to $q_{\mathrm{FO}}(W)$ is correct on both states. Reliable assessment exists only if $\Phi$ factors through $q_{\mathrm{FO}}$.

Proof:

A deterministic evaluator receives the same registered input for $x$ and $y$, hence must output the same verdict. The target requires different verdicts. For stochastic evaluators with identical registered states and transition kernels, the output distributions are equal, so universal discrimination is impossible.

## 7.4 Local-stability corollary

> **COR-LOCAL-STABILITY.** For deterministic first-order-local $L$, if two latent states share the same first-order registration and receive the same inputs, then every finite iterate of $L$ remains identical. Local contraction can stabilize the common projection but cannot recreate a distinction removed by registration.

## 7.5 Reflective necessity theorem

> **T-REFLECTIVE-NECESSITY.** When a target depends on process identity, function contract, composition, provenance, warrant, participant consequence, or successor obligation, a representation containing only domain concepts and conclusions is inadequate whenever it collapses process structures with different target verdicts.

## 7.6 Nominal and certified connectivity

Let:

- $P_{\mathrm{nom}}$ be nominal target-relevant paths;
- $P_{\mathrm{cert}}$ be paths preserving target-relevant semantic invariants;
- $P_{\mathrm{false}}=P_{\mathrm{nom}}\setminus P_{\mathrm{cert}}$;
- $P_{\mathrm{req}}$ be required target paths.

Define:

$$
\Psi=\frac{|P_{\mathrm{false}}|}{|P_{\mathrm{nom}}|},
\qquad
\rho_{\mathrm{sem}}=\frac{|P_{\mathrm{cert}}|}{|P_{\mathrm{req}}|}.
$$

Nominal connectivity can increase while certified semantic reach decreases.

## 7.7 Countermodels

The Supplement must include:

- a local target fully determined by $q_{\mathrm{FO}}$;
- a first-order projection preserving all target-relevant distinctions;
- a reflective registry that adds no new spanning composition;
- a target-aware monitor that abstains without increasing cognitive order;
- an equivalent renaming that creates no new semantic path.

## 8.1 Participant-indexed architecture

For participant $i$, distinguish:

- signal carrier $\Sigma_i$;
- conceptual FSS $X_i$;
- memory $M_i$;
- target space $T_i$;
- participant-indexed fitness/continuation space $F_i$;
- world-registration state $W_i$;
- certificate space $C_i$;
- residual space $R_i$.

## 8.2 Canonical function registry

Boundary functions:

- **Register**: admit typed external signal structure into an FSS without minting unavailable distinctions.
- **Express**: encode selected FSS structure onto a typed carrier while recording loss.

Conceptual functions:

- **Store**: preserve recoverable identity, relation, verdict, obligations, and provenance.
- **Recall**: reactivate or reconstruct stored structure under target and budget.
- **$L$**: evaluate or transform within active local support.
- **$G$**: evaluate or transform across globally relevant support while preserving successor obligations.
- **Promotion/Create**: preserve an admissible composite as a reusable higher-order operand.

External control:

- **Choice**: select the next external-function invocation through the internal fitness-control basis.

Internal fitness-control basis:

- Model;
- Evaluate;
- Stability;
- Adaptation;
- Decomposition;

Parallelism is therefore observational commutation, not simultaneous wall-clock execution.

# 9. Cognitive order, target-relative certification, and representation invariance

## 9.1 Composition identity

A registered composition is:

$$

with identity, signature, domain, codomain, boundary conditions, execution semantics, and preserved invariants.

## 9.2 Functional equivalence

For target language $\mathcal L$ and admissible domain $D$, two compositions are equivalent when they induce the same target-relevant transformation and residual status under every admissible input:

$$
\Gamma_1\simeq_{D,\mathcal L}\Gamma_2
\iff
\forall x\in D,
\operatorname{Obs}_{\mathcal L}(\Gamma_1x)
=
\operatorname{Obs}_{\mathcal L}(\Gamma_2x).
$$

Mere renaming, reassociation, redundant insertion, or implementation-level call expansion is equivalent unless it changes the registered contract.

## 9.3 Span

A composition spans conceptual space for the architecture's declared universal target family $\mathcal T^*$ when it can connect every target-required region through certified transformations, preserve semantic identity and successor obligations, and return correct or calibrated-undetermined verdicts within the architecture's admissibility and resource contract.

For finite assessment, define:

$$
\operatorname{SpanCert}_{T,\epsilon,B}(\Gamma,\mathcal C).
$$

This certificate is indexed by target subfamily $T$, residual tolerance $\epsilon$, and resource budget $B$. It is evidence about span, not the definition of cognitive order itself.

## 9.4 Canonical cognitive order

Let:

$$
S(\mathcal C)
=
\left\{
[\Gamma]_{\simeq}:
\Gamma\text{ is registered, reusable, nonredundant, and spans }\mathcal T^*
\right\}.

Then:

$$
\operatorname{Ord}(\mathcal C)=|S(\mathcal C)|.
$$

> **Cognitive order.** A cognition is of order $N$ when it has $N$ functionally non-equivalent compositions of the external functions of cognition and each composition spans conceptual space.

## 9.5 Finite order certificate

A finite assessment reports:

$$
\widehat{\operatorname{Ord}}_{T,\epsilon,B}(\mathcal C)
=
\left|
\left\{
[\Gamma]_{\simeq}:
\operatorname{SpanCert}_{T,\epsilon,B}(\Gamma,\mathcal C)
\right\}
\right|.
$$

The result is:

- a lower bound on intrinsic order when $T\subseteq\mathcal T^*$;
- exact only when target completeness and certificate completeness are proved;
- undetermined when the assessment cannot establish completeness.

This distinction answers the target/budget relativity objection without replacing the canonical definition.

## 9.6 Representation invariance theorem

Construct and prove:

The proof should induce a bijection between $S(\mathcal C)$ and $S(\mathcal C')$.

This theorem supports the finite implementation without claiming general decidability for unbounded cognition.

# 10. Fragmentation, reversal, and recurrence: strengthened theorem package

## 10.1 Obligation arrivals and integration service

Let $D_N(t)$ be cumulative target-weighted coherence obligations arriving by time $t$ at fixed order $N$. Let $B_N(t)$ be cumulative obligations discharged by existing span-certified compositions under the declared resources. Define backlog:

$$
U_N(t)=\max\{0,D_N(t)-B_N(t)\}.
$$

The model should distinguish:

- arrival rate $a_N(t)=D_N(t+1)-D_N(t)$;
- service rate $s_N(t)=B_N(t+1)-B_N(t)$;
- residual severity weights;
- obligation dependencies;
- locality of generated obligations.

## 10.2 Backlog divergence theorem

> **T-UFRAG-QUEUE.** If
> $$
> \limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{D_N(t)-B_N(t)}{t}>0,
> $$
> or more generally if cumulative arrival exceeds cumulative certified service by an unbounded amount, then $U_N(t)$ is unbounded along a subsequence.

This is the precise mathematical result. The theorem must not call the conclusion regional fragmentation until locality assumptions are added.

## 10.3 Regional fragmentation assumptions

To derive locally coherent regions, define an obligation graph $H_t$ whose nodes are conceptual regions and whose edges are certified or unresolved cross-region obligations. Assume:

- local integration keeps intra-region residual below $\epsilon_{\mathrm{local}}$;
- cross-region obligation arrivals exceed certified cross-region service;
- unresolved cross-region edges are not replaced by false semantic identities;
- region growth does not collapse all regions into one target-independent component.

## 10.4 Regional fragmentation theorem

> **T-REGIONAL-FRAG.** Under the regional assumptions, there exists a sequence of times at which the conceptual FSS contains at least two regions that are internally certified within $\epsilon_{\mathrm{local}}$ but whose required cross-region relation remains unresolved or incorrectly conflated. Nominal connectivity may remain high.

This theorem carries the claim "locally coherent but globally incompatible regions."

## 10.5 Reflective-loss theorem

Let $C_t$ be the set of target-different pairs collapsed by the first-order projection, with target weights $w_t(x,y)$. Define unavoidable conflation burden:

$$
Q_t=\sum_{(x,y)\in C_t}w_t(x,y).
$$

> **T-SEM-FRAG.** If $Q_t$ is unbounded and no additional target-relevant distinctions are registered, then the lower bound on unavoidable first-order error or calibrated-undetermined mass grows according to the chosen decision loss.

The weighting and loss function must be explicit.

## 10.6 Reversal theorem

Suppose an added composition $\Gamma_{N+1}$ changes service capacity from $B_N$ to $B_{N+1}$ and discharges the current frontier within residual $\epsilon$:

$$
D_N(t)-B_{N+1}(t)\leq\epsilon
$$

throughout the declared interval.

> **T-REVERSAL.** The order lift changes the current regime from increasing backlog to bounded residual over that interval.

This is a substantive theorem about dynamics, not the cardinality definition of order.

## 10.7 Recurrence theorem

Let the lift enlarge the admissible operand domain, creating a new arrival process $D_{N+1}(t)$.

> **T-RECURRENCE.** If the new obligation arrivals eventually exceed order-$(N+1)$ certified service by an unbounded amount and no further spanning composition or scaling change occurs, then $U_{N+1}(t)$ becomes unbounded along a subsequence.

## 10.8 Countermodels

Required countermodels include:

- bounded domain;
- same-order service scaling that matches arrivals;
- target family that ignores the unresolved distinctions;
- distributed architecture whose branching increases service faster than obligations;
- finite interval with temporary backlog decrease;
- reflection that improves abstention but not service capacity;
- false nominal bridges that increase connectivity without reducing certified backlog.

A candidate search or comparison functional may be:

$$
S_{\mathrm{CST}}(W)
=
S_{\mathrm{deform}}
+S_{\mathrm{resource}}
+S_{\mathrm{uncertainty}}
+S_{\mathrm{residual}}.
$$

Fatal logical defects are excluded by admissibility, not assigned finite penalties.

Before use, prove or test:

- nonnegativity;
- invariance under admissible reparameterization;
- zero or minimum value on exact preserving transformations;
- stability under equivalent encodings;
- no reversal of hard logical status;
- comparative utility beyond direct typed graph formalization.

## 14.10 Lean-facing representation

CST should expose:

- structures and dependent types;
- semantic equivalence relations;
- admissibility propositions;
- path and transport operations;
- theorem parameters for external mappings;
- proof obligations for every claimed preservation law.

A possible Lean-facing semantic-equivalence definition is:

```lean
structure FSS where
  Node : Type
  Edge : Type
  nodeType : Node -> TypeTag
  edgeType : Edge -> EdgeTag
  src : Edge -> Node
  dst : Edge -> Node
  dist : Node -> Node -> Real
  boundary : BoundaryData Node Edge
  admissible : Transition Node Edge -> Prop

structure AdmissibleIso (X Y : FSS) where
  nodeMap : X.Node -> Y.Node
  edgeMap : X.Edge -> Y.Edge
  preservesTypes : Prop
  preservesIncidence : Prop
  preservesMetric : Prop
  preservesBoundary : Prop
  preservesContracts : Prop

def SemanticallyEquivalent (X Y : FSS) (x : X.Node) (y : Y.Node) : Prop :=
  Exists fun F : AdmissibleIso X Y => F.nodeMap x = y
```

The final implementation may refine these fields, but the semantics must remain structural rather than label-based.

## 14.11 CST success criteria

CST succeeds as a formal representation if:

1. every target-relevant manuscript object has a mathematical representation;
2. equivalent encodings yield equivalent Lean objects and theorem status;
3. semantic mutations alter the expected theorem dependencies;
4. unresolved external mappings remain explicit parameters;
5. independent receivers can use the artifact on hidden claims;
6. the representation is more compact, reliable, or transferable than prose-only and registry-only conditions.

## 14.12 String-specific defeat conditions

The string-specific layer is narrowed or rejected if:

- the induced metric is arbitrary relative to FSS semantics;
- equivalent encodings yield unstable semantic identity;
- the worldsheet adds no useful structure beyond a typed graph;
- holonomy fails to detect known drift;
- the action ranks logically invalid objects as preferable;
- registration cost recreates the original propagation problem;
- direct Lean formalization performs equally well with lower complexity.

Failure of the string-specific layer does not defeat the core FMI-CNS mathematics or the Lean proof package.

# 29. Conclusion

v15 should not attempt to solve propagation by building a new general-purpose reasoning engine. It should formalize the semantic object that natural language currently fails to transmit reliably and use Lean to check the resulting mathematics.

The central positive claim is that semantic identity in an FSS is constituted by metric position, typed interactions, and admissible relational configuration.
